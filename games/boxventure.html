About the game

I think everyone had an imaginary friend as a kid, and went on the wildest adventures with them. That's exactly what Boxventure is about. Tina is three years old and always has her handmade, hand painted friend Bobby by her side. Together they go on adventures that could only be this colorful and boundless inside a child's imagination.

Cardboard boxes, pens, paper, anything you'd find in a kid's room or out in the garden becomes the backdrop for the next big adventure. In Boxventure you help Bobby take bold leaps, climb up heights, and explore this handmade world. It's calm, playful, and full of little details, exactly the kind of thing you'd want for a cozy break.

The story behind it

As a kid I was constantly building little figures out of cardboard, out of whatever I had lying around, and inventing entire worlds and adventures to go with them. That memory is really where Boxventure comes from. My goal was never to design some super clever platformer mechanic, it was to bring back a feeling: what it's like when a toy you built yourself suddenly comes to life and goes on an adventure with you.

That's how Tina and Bobby came to be. Tina is kind of a stand in for that childlike part of all of us, the part that just builds its own world without rules or limits, using whatever's within reach. That's also why the world around Bobby is built exactly the way a kid would build it: out of cardboard, paper, and anything else you can put together. Nothing is perfect or polished, everything looks a little handmade, and honestly that's exactly where the charm comes from for me.

Boxventure is my counterpart to the horror games I usually make. Where those are all about tension and unease, here I wanted to create the exact opposite: a place that doesn't ask anything of you except a bit of time and curiosity. I didn't just want to give players another platformer, I wanted to give them back a little piece of that childlike imagination that's way too easy to lose as an adult.
